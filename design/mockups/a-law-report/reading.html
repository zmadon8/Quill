A. Arbitrability

1. Fraud

  1. Mere, but not serious, allegations of fraud arbitrable - (a) A. Ayyasamy [(2016) 10 SCC 386] ; (b) Ameet Lalchand Shah [(2018) 15 SCC 678]⎘↗
  2. Serious issue of fraud involving criminal wrongdoing not arbitrable - N. Radhakrishnan [(2010) 1 SCC 72]⎘↗
  3. Cannot file S. 11 when allegation that agreement procured by fraud - India Household [(2007) 5 SCC 510] For review⎘↗

2. General

  1. Rights in rem not arbitrable. Provides non-exhaustive list - Booz Allen [(2011) 5 SCC 532]⎘↗
  2. Trust Deed not arbitrable - Vimal Kishor Shah v. Jayesh Dinesh Shah [(2016) 8 SCC 788]⎘↗
  3. Issue of Limitation of the claim can be decided by the arbitrator - BSNL v. Nortel Networks (2021) 5 SCC 738 @ paras 40-47 & 53 Overruled⎘↗
  4. Categories of disputes held non-arbitrable
    • Criminal offences of a public nature
    • Matrimonial disputes and guardianship
    • Insolvency and winding-up
    ⎘↗

B. Letters Patent & OS Rules

1. Clause 12: Leave to Sue

(a) Ex Parte / Without Notice

  1. Leave under Clause 12 may be granted ex parte, but the defendant may apply to revoke it - Indian Mineral & Chemicals Co. v. Deutsche Bank (2004) 12 SCC 376⎘↗
  2. Plaint must disclose all facts bearing on jurisdiction at the time of seeking leave - Sharda Devi v. Mohan Lal (1989) 2 Cal LJ 41⎘↗

(b) Determining Jurisdiction

  1. Part of cause of action within local limits is enough to found jurisdiction - Oil & Natural Gas Commission v. Utpal Kumar Basu (1994) 4 SCC 711⎘↗
  2. Cause of action means every fact necessary for the plaintiff to prove to obtain a decree - Rajasthan High Court Advocates Assn. v. Union of India (2001) 2 SCC 294 For review⎘↗
  3. Leave is not a mere formality; the court must be satisfied on the pleadings - Kusum Ingots v. Union of India (2004) 6 SCC 254⎘↗
